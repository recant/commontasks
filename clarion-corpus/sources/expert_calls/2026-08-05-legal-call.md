# Legal call: Rachel Goldman — 2026-08-05

**Attendees:** [[people/rachel-goldman]] (Goldman Health Law); [[people/elena-vasquez]], [[people/priya-nair]], [[people/dana-ortiz]] (Clarion)
**Context:** TK-1130 — two [[clients/harbor-family-medicine]] denials from [[payers/unitedhealth]]; SOP-007's 60-day reconsideration window would force a write-off on the older claim (day 71 today). Question: does the 60-day rule apply to delegated-credentialing contracts?
**Notes:** Elena

---

**Elena Vasquez:** Rachel, the facts: two Harbor claims denied on EOBs dated June 18 and May 26 — $12,380 and $9,840. Our SOP says UHC reconsiderations run 60 days from the remittance date, which kills the older one. Dana flagged that Harbor is on a delegated-credentialing agreement and asked whether the 60 actually applies. That's the question.

**Rachel Goldman:** It's the right question, and the answer is that the reconsideration window isn't one number at UHC — it depends entirely on which network agreement the provider participates under. The 60-day window everyone quotes is UHC's standard administrative policy for non-delegated participating providers. It's in their provider manual, which is why it ends up in everyone's SOPs.

**Rachel Goldman:** But delegated-credentialing arrangements are governed by the participation agreement itself, and I reviewed Clarion's delegated template before this call. UHC delegated-credentialing contracts: 90-day reconsideration window per §7.3. Section 7.3 — "Provider Disputes and Reconsiderations" — expressly provides ninety days from the date of the remittance advice, and as negotiated contract language it controls over the provider-manual default. Your May 26 claim is at day 71. Under §7.3 it is timely, with 19 days of margin.

**Priya Nair:** So the SOP's 60 wasn't wrong when written — it's wrong for the clients we've since moved to delegated agreements.

**Rachel Goldman:** Precisely. The 60-day figure remains correct for any standard, non-delegated contract. The failure mode is applying the manual default to a negotiated agreement — in your favor here, since the contract is more generous, but I've seen it cut the other way on different terms.

**Elena Vasquez:** Practical mechanics for filing the older claim?

**Rachel Goldman:** Three things. File through the delegated-portal dispute queue, not the standard reconsideration channel — delegated disputes route differently and the standard queue may auto-reject on the 60-day timer. Cite §7.3 explicitly in the cover letter, quote the ninety-day language. And attach my memo — I'll memorialize this in writing to you tomorrow so the file shows a legal basis, not an ops opinion.

**Dana Ortiz:** And going forward — how do we stop the next person from trusting the SOP?

**Rachel Goldman:** Update the SOP with both windows keyed to contract type, and — this is the part people skip — inventory which of your clients are on delegated agreements so the ops team can look it up instead of guessing. If the roster isn't clean, that's a half-day project that prevents the next $10,000 question.

**Elena Vasquez:** Both noted and mine to own. Decision for the record: we file both Harbor reconsiderations under §7.3 — including the day-71 claim — tomorrow with your memo attached.
