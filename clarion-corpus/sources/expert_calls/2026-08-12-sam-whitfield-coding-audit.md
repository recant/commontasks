# Expert call: Dr. Sam Whitfield — 2026-08-12

**Attendees:** [[people/sam-whitfield]] (Whitfield Compliance Group, MD CPC); [[people/elena-vasquez]], [[people/priya-nair]] (Clarion)
**Context:** External coding-compliance review commissioned by Elena (per the 5/6 ops sync follow-up). Whitfield analyzed a stratified 240-encounter sample of established-patient E/M claims across four Clarion clients, with documentation excerpts.
**Notes:** Elena

---

**Dr. Sam Whitfield:** Method first, findings second. I pulled a stratified sample — 240 established-patient office visits across your four highest-E/M-volume clients — and scored the code-level distributions against specialty benchmarks. And to keep this clean: I ran the sample and wrote my findings before reading your internal notes, so what I'm about to say is my read of the data, not an echo of anyone here.

**Dr. Sam Whitfield:** The headline: [[clients/harbor-family-medicine]] bills 99214 — the level-four established visit — on 68% of the sampled encounters. The family-medicine benchmark for that code sits around 44%. A 24-point overweight on level fours, with correspondingly thin 99213 volume, is exactly the distribution shape that payer audit-targeting algorithms select for. I want to be careful: distribution is a *screen*, not a verdict — some practices genuinely run complex panels. But this screen is loud.

**Elena Vasquez:** For context, our ops lead flagged the same shape internally in May — that Harbor was running roughly 20 points above benchmark.

**Dr. Sam Whitfield:** Then your instruments agree, which should raise everyone's confidence that the signal is real. The question the distribution can't answer is whether the documentation supports the levels. Under the 2021 E/M framework that's a medical-decision-making question — MDM first, time as the alternative path. My recommendation: a 60-encounter documentation audit of Harbor's level-four visits, MDM-scored by the 2021 guidelines, before Q4. That's enough volume to be defensible and small enough to finish in a month.

**Priya Nair:** And if the documentation comes back thin — what's the guidance? I want to head off anyone panicking into blanket downcoding.

**Dr. Sam Whitfield:** Correct instinct. Do not blanket-downcode — systematic downcoding without documentation review is itself a compliance red flag, and it forfeits legitimate revenue where the medicine really was level-four. If the audit finds under-documentation, the fix is documentation improvement and provider education, code corrections only where the record truly can't support the level. Fix the records, not the ratio.

**Elena Vasquez:** The other three clients in the sample?

**Dr. Sam Whitfield:** Unremarkable. [[clients/cedar-point-pediatrics]] in particular is textbook — their pediatric E/M curve matches the specialty benchmark within noise. Nothing actionable outside Harbor.

**Elena Vasquez:** Then the plan: I'll open the audit engagement this week — 60 Harbor encounters, MDM-first scoring, target completion September 15, findings to you for co-review. Amy Tran gets a heads-up framed as proactive quality work, which it is.

**Dr. Sam Whitfield:** Agreed on all counts. Send the sample pull methodology past me before you draw it — stratification matters if we want the result to stand up.
